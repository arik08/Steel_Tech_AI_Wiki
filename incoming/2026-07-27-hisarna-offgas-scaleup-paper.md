# HIsarna off-gas scale-up paper

Hosseini et al., “Off-Gas System Scale-Up of HIsarna Iron-Making Process: A
CFD-Based Approach,” Metallurgical and Materials Transactions B 53 (2022)
3557–3574, was published 2 September 2022
(DOI 10.1007/s11663-022-02620-4).

The paper describes HIsarna as a combination of a cyclone converter furnace
(CCF) and HIsmelt-derived smelting reduction vessel (SRV). Fine ore and oxygen
enter the cyclone; post-combustion supplies heat for pre-reduction and melting,
and molten droplets fall to the SRV for final coal-based reduction in slag.
The paper reports the IJmuiden pilot as nominally 60,000 tonnes hot metal per
year and discusses then-proposed 400,000 t/y demonstration and 1 Mt/y
industrial scales. Those scale values are design/scale-up cases, not achieved
Jamshedpur production.

The CFD study validates a pilot-scale model against measured temperatures,
heat loss and gas composition, then uses it to design a larger reflux chamber.
It identifies off-gas post-combustion, oxygen-port geometry and flow, residence
time, wall heat loss, refractory thickness/erosion, accretion, quench and gas
cleaning as scale-up variables. Inspection data cited in the paper showed
average reflux-chamber refractory thickness of roughly 32–38 mm versus
49.5 mm when fresh near the hot oxygen-lance region, illustrating a concrete
maintenance and model-validation risk.
