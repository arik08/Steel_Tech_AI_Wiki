The programme began in 2016 with more than EUR 60 million of federal support.
The Duisburg pilot started in March 2018 and produced ammonia, methanol and
higher alcohols from steelworks process gases. It included a 2 MW alkaline
water electrolyzer. Phase two received EUR 75 million through 2024 to test
long-duration stability, scale-up and transfer to other industries.

The third phase received EUR 50 million through 2028 and focuses on
application-oriented validation, direct-reduction gas adaptation, methanol and
hydrogen. It is a carbon-utilization research and demonstration programme; it
does not constitute permanent geological storage of BF CO2.
