Source evidence excerpt collected 2026-07-25.

Humbert and co-authors compare hydrogen reduction with aqueous hydroxide,
molten salt, and molten oxide electrolysis as mature hypothetical green-steel
systems. Their MOE estimate is a prospective techno-economic model, not measured
commercial-plant performance.

The paper estimates MOE specific electricity consumption at approximately
2.89-4.45 kWh/kg Fe by scaling aluminium-electrolysis data, and cites an
independent inside-out estimate of about 4.7-4.78 kWh/kg Fe. Electricity is
identified as the dominant operating-cost driver. The authors estimate MOE-cell
capital cost from aluminium smelters and warn that the low maturity of iron
electrolysis makes the estimates likely to understate actual cost.

The analysis says MOE can accept fines and lumps because ore dissolves in the
molten oxide bath, but gangue/electrolyte handling remains unresolved. Selling
spent electrolyte or slag as a cement product is presented as an unproven idea
and is excluded from the base economics. Oxygen sales are also judged unlikely
to transform economics at large scale because of market saturation.

Citation: M.S. Humbert et al., Journal of Sustainable Metallurgy 10,
1679-1701 (2024). DOI: 10.1007/s40831-024-00878-3.
