# Fortescue low-temperature DER core-research final report

This 65-page final report, dated 2025-12-22, records Stage 1 results from a
Fortescue, Deakin University, and Curtin University research program funded by
the Australian Renewable Energy Agency. The process is a solid-state slurry
electrolyser using an alkaline electrolyte and Pilbara iron ore below 130
degrees Celsius.

The report states that real-ore tests exposed a gap between synthetic
iron-oxide chemistry and operable ore conversion. Fine particles could
increase Faradaic efficiency in an analytical rig but also increased adhesion
and fouling, while benchtop tests did not show a simple particle-size trend.
High NaOH concentration was necessary for stable reduction of Pilbara ore;
lower concentrations produced partial or incomplete conversion. Competing
hydrogen evolution, membrane robustness and conductivity, electrode stability,
mass transport, and ore-impurity effects remained development constraints.

In benchtop tests using 3.2 wt% Pilbara ore at 100 degrees Celsius, the reported
Faradaic efficiencies were approximately 4%, 28%, and 69% at 30, 40, and
60 wt% NaOH respectively. These are condition-specific laboratory results,
not a plant-wide efficiency guarantee.

Membrane-screening tables report test-specific electricity use of roughly
6.2–9.9 kWh per kilogram of iron with Faradaic efficiency around 32–53% for
the compared membrane cases. One selected membrane test maintained operation
for four hours without detected crossover. These short-duration screening
results should not be represented as commercial energy intensity or proven
membrane life.

Stage 1 was being finalised as of August 2025. Stage 2 is intended to design,
construct, and commission an electrolyser unit and balance of plant. The
defined Stage 2 demonstration objective is at least 50% conversion to magnetite
and/or metallic iron with a 100 kg feed process and achievement of TRL 6 after
construction and commissioning. The report therefore documents an R&D
benchmark and scale-up objective, not an achieved TRL 6 demonstration.
