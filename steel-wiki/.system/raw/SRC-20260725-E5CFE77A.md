# Western Australia DWER BioIron works approval decision report

Western Australia Department of Water and Environmental Regulation works approval W6964/2024/1 decision report, dated 28 July 2025, describes the proposed BioIron pilot before its later construction pause.

The proposed linear hearth furnace pilot was designed for around one tonne of iron product per operating hour and approximately 2,000 operating hours per year. Campaigns were expected to last eight weeks, followed by three-week shutdowns for modifications.

Feed preparation included milling, storage, mixing, green briquetting, screening, and recycling of briquette fines. The proposed feed was Pilbara fine iron ore plus wheat straw or sawdust biomass, fluxes such as limestone/dolomite/magnesite, and graphite additive. Nitrogen was used for inertisation and natural gas for start-up, off-gas combustion assistance, and mould heating.

The BioIron furnace was divided into a conventional pre-reduction/preheating section and a microwave section. Oxygen-depleted warm process gas was recycled to the preheat zone. Air lances partially or completely oxidised pyrolysis products to control top-space temperature.

A motor-driven compactor roll transferred briquettes to the microwave section, homogenised bed depth across the pan, and helped prevent gas and dust ingress.

In the microwave zone, briquettes were converted into highly metallised, carbon-containing DRI. Up to twelve microwave horns distributed energy across the conveyor width, supplied by microwave generators through waveguides. Seals controlled microwave leakage and ambient-air cooling protected the hood shell.

DRI discharged to a screw cooler, then silos for 60-72 hours of air/nitrogen passivation. Cold DRI could be stored or later briquetted. An induction furnace melted DRI fines to iron and slag, producing 15.8 kg cast ingots in the pilot plan.

Process off-gas could be partially or fully combusted inside the pre-reduction zone. Remaining unburned hydrocarbons and particulates required a burner, spray cooler, dilution air, baghouse, fan, and stack.

The plan estimated about 700 tonnes per year of slag, 168 tonnes per year of biomass dust, 72 tonnes per year of furnace off-gas dust, and 675 tonnes per year of off-specification DRI. These were permit-design estimates, not achieved operating results.
