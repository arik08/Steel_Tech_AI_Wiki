Source evidence excerpt collected 2026-07-25.

Allanore, Yin, and Sadoway report that molten oxide electrolysis directly
produces liquid metal from oxide feedstock. For carbon-free operation it needs
an anode that resists consumption while evolving oxygen. Iron production poses
three severe materials conditions: temperature above iron's 1538 degrees
Celsius melting point, anodic corrosion of most metals, and spontaneous
reduction of iron oxide on contact with many refractory metals and carbon.

The study demonstrated chromium-based alloy anodes with limited consumption
during iron extraction and oxygen evolution. Stability was attributed to an
electronically conductive chromium(III)-oxide/aluminium-oxide solid solution in
a corundum structure that formed a protective layer. The authors concluded that
this material enabled larger-scale evaluation, while the journal's editorial
summary explicitly noted that scaling and long-term performance still required
assessment.

Citation: Antoine Allanore, Lan Yin, Donald R. Sadoway, "A new anode material
for oxygen evolution in molten oxide electrolysis", Nature 497, 353-356 (2013).
DOI: 10.1038/nature12134.
