# Suppression of Surface Hot Shortness due to Cu in Recycled Steels

Shibata and co-authors explain that scrap-borne copper can cause surface hot
shortness during hot deformation and that tin accelerates the copper effect.
Preferential oxidation removes iron at the steel/scale interface, leaving a
liquid copper-rich phase that can penetrate austenite grain boundaries and
create surface cracking.

The paper evaluates suppression through reduced oxidation, incorporation of
the Cu-rich phase into scale, back-diffusion into the steel matrix, and
restriction of liquid penetration. Silicon, Mn, S with Mn, and B reduced
susceptibility in the reported tests; heating and deformation conditions also
affected cracking. These are product/process tolerance measures and do not
constitute removal of Cu or Sn from the steel recycling loop.
