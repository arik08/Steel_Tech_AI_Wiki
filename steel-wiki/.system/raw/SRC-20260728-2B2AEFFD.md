# Mohanta et al. 2026 — real-time HPSR metallisation diagnostics

- Title: Electrical and Spectroscopic Diagnostics as Real-Time Metallization Indicators During Hydrogen Plasma Smelting Reduction
- Journal: Advanced Sustainable Systems, volume 10, issue 1, article e01578
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/adsu.202501578
- Publisher page: https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/adsu.202501578
- NLR record: https://research-hub.nlr.gov/en/publications/electrical-and-spectroscopic-diagnostics-as-real-time-metallizati/
- Collected: 2026-07-28
- Peer review: NLR classifies the record as a peer-reviewed journal article.

## Experimental scope

- About 10 g of DRI-grade hematite was reduced in a 7 kW transferred-arc
  reactor at 100, 150 and 200 A in Ar–5% H2 at about 0.9 bar.
- Arc voltage was sampled at 500 Hz and spatially selective optical-emission
  spectra were collected close to the plasma–metal interface.
- Reduction was interrupted at 200 s intervals so that mass loss and
  metallisation could be measured ex situ for comparison with the electrical
  and optical signals.

## Main diagnostic results

- Arc voltage initially fell by about 15–20% as oxygen removal increased plasma
  conductivity, and fell again near about 95% metallisation as the arc coupled
  to electrically conductive metallic iron and iron vapour increased plasma
  conductivity.
- The strong Fe I 526.95 nm line became self-absorbed and could underestimate
  iron-vapour density.
- Weaker Fe I lines at 404.58 and 438.35 nm, normalised to Ar I 696.5 nm and
  combined with O I 615.6 nm, formed a metallisation index that tracked measured
  progress most reliably through the intermediate range.
- The paper reports good agreement chiefly from about 10% to 80–85%
  metallisation. Above roughly 80%, even the weaker iron lines became
  self-absorbed and the index fluctuated.

## Interpretation boundary

- The work demonstrates a promising non-invasive monitoring principle at bench
  scale; it does not establish an industrial closed-loop control system.
- The voltage signal is affected by current, arc mode, iron evaporation and
  conductive coupling, so a simple voltage threshold is not a universal
  metallisation measurement.
- The optical index has explicit low- and high-metallisation limits, and the
  near-interface plasma can become optically thick and non-LTE.
