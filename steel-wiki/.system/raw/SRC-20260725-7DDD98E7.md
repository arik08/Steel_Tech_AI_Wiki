# Image-Segmentation-Guided Fragmentized Steel Scrap Tramp Material Characterization

Quan and co-authors investigate whether RGB computer vision can estimate the
copper contamination class of commercially graded fragmentized steel scrap.
They use visible copper-bearing objects such as wires, motor rotors, and coils,
not copper dissolved invisibly in the steel matrix.

UK fragmentized grades 3A and 3B have BMRA copper limits of 0.20 wt% and
0.25 wt%, respectively. Grade 3B represents about 28.5% of UK steel scrap in
the cited dataset. End-of-life vehicle shredding mixes steel body fragments
with copper wiring and other materials; some contaminants remain attached or
entangled even after conventional separation.

XRF and LIBS provide accurate spot measurements, but high-throughput bulk
composition estimation is difficult because of acquisition time, surface
condition, piece-to-piece variation, and the need to connect a detected
fragment with its mass contribution. Inline systems are therefore more mature
for segregation or grade assignment than for precise bulk Cu measurement.

The paper's image-segmentation-guided classifier achieved 86.67% accuracy in
assigning images to the correct copper composition class on its curated
dataset. This is evidence of laboratory feasibility, not a production-line
assurance that RGB images alone can continuously measure bulk chemistry.
