# Rio Tinto BioIron small-scale pilot, 2022

Rio Tinto announced on 23 November 2022 that BioIron had been tested extensively over 18 months in a small-scale pilot plant in Germany by Rio Tinto, Metso Outotec, and the University of Nottingham Microwave Process Engineering Group.

The pilot used batches of 1,000 golf-ball-sized briquettes made from Pilbara iron ore fines and lignocellulosic biomass. Raw biomass acted as reductant and microwave energy drove reduction to metallic iron.

Rio Tinto planned a specially designed continuous pilot plant with a capacity of one tonne per hour. Candidate biomass included wheat straw, canola stalks, barley straw, sugar-cane bagasse, and purpose-grown crops.

The biomass and iron ore are blended. Heating is supplied by a combination of combustible gases released from biomass and high-efficiency microwaves. Rio Tinto described sustainable biomass supply as complex and ruled out sources supporting old-growth or High Conservation Value forest logging.

The release described near-zero or potentially net-negative emissions only as potential outcomes dependent on renewable power, biomass carbon circulation, and, for net-negative operation, carbon capture and storage.
