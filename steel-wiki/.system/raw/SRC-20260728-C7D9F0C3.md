# Kim, Kang & Yi (2026) — 1173 K molten-oxide electrolysis notes

- Primary article: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-58521-y
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-58521-y
- Independent bibliographic cross-check: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42414383/
- Author-institution publication list: https://kang-lab.snu.ac.kr/publications/scie-papers
- Published: 2026-07-07 in *Scientific Reports*; article-in-press/unedited version at collection.
- Authors: Joongseok Kim, Jungshin Kang, Kyung-Woo Yi.
- Funding disclosed by PubMed: Korea Institute of Energy Technology Evaluation and Planning and POSCO.

## Reported experiment and result

- B2O3-Na2O molten oxide at 1173 K.
- Applied cell voltage: 1.6 V.
- Pure Fe oxides in air: current efficiency 65.50-68.72%.
- Pure Fe oxides in Ar: 67.49-78.56%, attributed to suppressed reoxidation under lower oxygen partial pressure.
- Ore tests in air:
  - hematite ore: 64.14%
  - goethite ore: 66.00%
  - magnetite ore: 67.58%
- Full article describes platinum electrodes and five-hour electrolysis tests; reduced Fe forms on/alloys with the solid Pt cathode rather than being tapped as a bulk liquid-iron product.

## Evidence boundary

- The result is a peer-reviewed laboratory electrolysis study and supports electrochemical feasibility at 1173 K.
- It does not establish continuous ore feeding, continuous metal removal, durable industrial inert-anode operation, refractory campaign life, tonne-scale throughput, product qualification, electricity use per tonne, plant CAPEX, or commercial readiness.
- The paper is not a Boston Metal operating report, despite addressing the broader molten-oxide-electrolysis family.
