# Properties evaluation of DRI smelting slag for molten iron production

This study examines CaO–SiO2–MgO–Al2O3–FeO slags representative of melting
low-quality DRI. It treats slag viscosity, sulfide capacity and iron–slag
separation as operating variables rather than assuming that all blast-furnace
slag practice transfers directly to an electric smelter.

The authors note that conventional DRI feed commonly requires at least 67% Fe
and less than 2% combined silica and alumina. Lower-quality DRI contains more
gangue and can have lower metallisation, leaving more FeO to reduce during
smelting. Basic fluxes such as CaO and MgO are needed to adjust the slag, but
additional flux increases slag mass and the energy required to heat it.

In the tested composition range, binary basicity
R = mass(CaO)/mass(SiO2) was varied from 0.4 to 1.2. Higher basicity generally
reduced viscosity and increased sulfide capacity. The authors conclude that a
basicity above 0.8 is needed for reasonable fluidity, desulfurisation and
slag–iron separation in the tested DRI-smelting slag. This is a laboratory
window for the tested chemistry, not a universal industrial set point.

The paper stresses that higher basicity consumes more CaO and increases slag
volume and energy consumption. It calls for integrated DRI–flux–carbon
experiments and validation of temperature, residence time, electrode heating,
periodic tapping and reductant-addition strategy.
