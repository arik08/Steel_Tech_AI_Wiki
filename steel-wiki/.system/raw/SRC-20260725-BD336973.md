# IEA Demand and Supply Measures for the Steel and Cement Transition

The IEA reported that announced near-zero-emissions iron-based steel capacity for
2030 was about 10 Mt, around 10% of the amount needed in that year on a net-zero
pathway. Disclosed offtake agreements represented a little under 2 Mt/year by
2030, with another 3 Mt of stated buyer demand not yet fulfilled.

Early near-zero steel and cement offerings were estimated to cost 10-125% more
than conventional production depending on region and technology. Policy measures
such as contracts for difference, public procurement, grants, tax credits and
enabling infrastructure can reduce first-mover risk.

Investment timing is critical because around 15% of the global steel production
fleet reaches a reinvestment decision around 2035. More than 200 Mt of
high-emissions conventional steelmaking capacity was planned or expected to come
online in the following years. New conventional assets therefore need a credible,
technically feasible conversion plan if they are described as near-zero capable.

Low-emissions hydrogen, electricity generation and grids, and CO2 transport and
storage are enabling infrastructure rather than optional externalities. Demand
aggregation and firm offtake agreements can help support final investment
decisions for large, site-specific projects.
