# Low temperature electrolysis for iron production via conductive colloidal electrode

Verified source: Royal Society of Chemistry, RSC Advances, 2015, 5, 5501–5507.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1039/C4RA14576C
Official article: https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2015/ra/c4ra14576c

Authors: Qiang Wang, Yi Zhu, Qiuyang Wu, Eric Gratz, Yan Wang.

The paper describes low-temperature iron production in a strong alkaline
aqueous electrolyte. Finely ground hematite particles are suspended in
concentrated NaOH solution and reduced to metallic iron at the cathode.

The reported experiment used a conductive colloidal electrode, 45 wt% NaOH,
110 °C, 1.7 V, a 1 cm electrode gap, and five hours of electrolysis. The
authors frame the conductive carbon network as a way to improve electrical
contact with suspended iron-oxide particles.

Evidence boundary: this is a laboratory electrochemical experiment, not a
continuous industrial plant or evidence for Electra's proprietary cell.
