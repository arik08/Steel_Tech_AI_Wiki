- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rser.2026.117058
- Authors: Zhen Wang; Mengmeng Liu; Renze Xu
- Issue date: September 2026
- Collected: 2026-07-28
- Peer review: journal review article

## Route comparison reported by the authors

- The review distinguishes molten-salt electrolysis, typically operated at
  1,073–1,223 K and producing solid or powdered iron, from conventional
  molten-oxide electrolysis above 1,823 K, which targets liquid iron.
- It identifies inert-anode durability, interfacial reaction kinetics,
  electrolyte conductivity and scale-up as continuing industrialization
  bottlenecks across the high-temperature electrolysis routes.

## Interpretation boundary

- The temperature and product-state ranges are route-level classifications in
  a review, not specifications for every cell design.
- The paper's comparison of theoretical energy and decarbonization potential
  does not establish demonstrated industrial efficiency, continuous operation,
  equipment life or commercial economics.
- The 1,173 K borate-electrolyte experiment reported separately by Kim et al.
  fits the lower-temperature solid-iron side of the taxonomy, and should not be
  merged with liquid-iron MOE demonstrations solely because both use the term
  molten oxide electrolysis.
