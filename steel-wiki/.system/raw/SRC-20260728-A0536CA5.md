## Verified experimental scope

The study used an 18 L AM200 continuous-flow arc-melting furnace at the Max
Planck Institute for Sustainable Materials. A tungsten electrode and the hearth
formed the DC arc. The experiments used a 200 A current, 10 mm arc length and
Ar-10 vol pct H2 plasma, with 2.5 to 12.5 minute batch durations. The feed was a
Metso hematite leach residue containing iron oxide plus Al, Mn and Cr oxides.

After 10 minutes, the authors calculated an 87 pct theoretical metallisation
degree and measured metal purity above 98 pct. The paper explicitly warns that
the metallisation calculation does not account for evaporated iron, so the
actual recovered metal amount is lower. Full metallisation was not necessarily
desirable in the small-scale setup because evaporation, oxide inclusion and
slag-function trade-offs increased.

Optical emission spectroscopy tracked changes in slag-element emissions,
plasma position, dust and electrode wear. Aluminium intensity was identified as
a possible indirect progress indicator, while tungsten emission showed that
electrode erosion can also be visible in the spectra.

## Scale-up limitations identified by the authors

- most HPSR work remains laboratory scale;
- refractory corrosion and slag optimisation need pilot-furnace validation;
- green hydrogen remains scarce and expensive;
- argon use must be reduced or recycled;
- off-gas recycling improves hydrogen use but adds capital cost;
- carburisation of HPSR metal still needs study;
- dust and arc motion can obstruct optical process monitoring;
- metal evaporation means theoretical metallisation is not equivalent to
  recovered-metal yield.
