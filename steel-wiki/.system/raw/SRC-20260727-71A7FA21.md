# European Commission INCITE status page for Volteron

The European Commission Joint Research Centre's INCITE page describes the
Volteron route as ore beneficiation and flotation followed by mixing
ultrafine ore with caustic soda and water. The circulating electrolyte is
electrolysed so that iron deposits at the cathode as metal plates and oxygen
is produced at the anode. The page states an aqueous electrolyte containing
50 wt% NaOH and an operating temperature of 110 degrees Celsius.

INCITE reports that a one-square-metre Volteron pilot cell was commissioned in
2025 and had been running since April 2025. It describes a row of four iron
plates, two cathodes, three anodes, and about 140 kg of metallic iron per row.
The narrative says the pilot was expected to validate TRL 7 by the end of
2025, while the structured status field labels the technique "Operational"
and "Achieved TRL 7." Because the narrative and status field use different
time framing, both should be retained rather than treating full industrial
scalability as proven.

The page gives an estimated overall energy-use range of 3.5–4.3 MWh per tonne
of iron plate and a 3.7 MWh/t target. Its data legend distinguishes estimated,
expected, pilot-monitored, and full-scale-monitored values; therefore the
range and target are not full-scale measured performance.

ArcelorMittal and John Cockerill planned engineering and a full CAPEX study for
a first scale-up plant using 40 kt/y modules, scalable to an 800 kt/y plant.
INCITE explicitly makes first-plant deployment dependent on pilot results,
framework conditions, and funding. It identifies demonstration-plant capital
risk and affordable electricity availability as barriers.
