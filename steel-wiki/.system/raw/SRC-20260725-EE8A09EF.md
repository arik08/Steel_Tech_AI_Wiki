# Optical spectra of hydrogen plasma smelting reduction

Pauna and coauthors published this open-access Steel Research International
article on 2024-05-15 (DOI 10.1002/srin.202400028). It evaluates optical
emission spectroscopy and image analysis at two laboratory reactors and the
K1-MET pilot reactor.

The monitoring targets include radiating atomic hydrogen, iron, oxygen, and
FeO. Plasma behavior is dynamic and the plasma-melt interaction is nonlinear,
so real-time process monitoring and control require more than nominal current
and voltage.

The K1-MET reactor specification lists maximum capacity of 90,000 g, a
refractory crucible, and continuous fine-ore charging at 100 to 200 g/min in
the reported campaign. The campaign used a steel premelt, argon and hydrogen
plasma, a hollow graphite electrode, roughly 500 A current, and fine ore with
66% total iron. These are experiment conditions, not a commercial design.

Water vapor and dust can absorb light and obstruct the optical path, which is
an important limitation for spectroscopy-based closed-loop control at scale.
