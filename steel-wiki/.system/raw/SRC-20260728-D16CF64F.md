## Reactor configuration evidence

Figure 1 depicts the HyREX reduction section as four physically separate
fluidized-bed reactor vessels arranged in a descending cascade. The cutaway of
each vessel shows a bundle of internal cyclone bodies and long internal diplegs
inside the reactor shell, rather than one external cyclone vessel beside each
reactor. External connections separately represent stage gas routing and the
bulk solids path.

The article text states that each HyREX fluidized-bed reactor has four
high-efficiency cyclones that recycle entrained ultrafine particles. It also
warns that elutriation losses and increasing cyclone load remain operating
issues. This supports showing four internal cyclones per reactor and treating
cyclone load as a scale-up KPI.

## Ore-reduction evidence

The study examines five hematite- or goethite-bearing sinter-feed ores under
non-static HyREX reduction conditions. Test particles were controlled to
1.0-1.7 mm for comparison. All five ores exceeded 90% final reduction degree in
the reported laboratory tests, but hematite-rich ore showed greater
disintegration. The authors conclude that ultrafines generated during
disintegration strongly affect final reduction degree and that the main
reduction stage is governed primarily by interfacial chemical reaction, whereas
the final stage slows under solid-state diffusion control.

These are laboratory results under selected HyREX reduction conditions, not
production guarantees for the 300,000 t/y Pohang demonstration plant.

## Diagram scope

The academic Figure 1 is a process schematic, not an as-built drawing, PFD or
P&ID. It supports reactor count, cascade arrangement, internal cyclone bundle,
general countercurrent gas/solids routing, and downstream ESF integration. It
does not disclose the demonstration plant's exact pressure profile, interstage
seal geometry, valve sequence, dimensions or control philosophy.
