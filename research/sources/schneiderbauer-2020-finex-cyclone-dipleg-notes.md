## Relevant evidence

The paper models an industrial-scale FINEX fluidized bed using process-scale
geometry and plant measurements. The modeled vessel is nearly cylindrical and
uses four cyclones to separate smaller particles entrained from the bed and
return them to the same fluidized bed.

Each cyclone has a dipleg below it. The paper explains that the dipleg prevents
gas from taking a short path into the cyclone through its lower solids outlet.
The simulation results likewise show the dipleg suppressing flow that would
otherwise enter the cyclone from below. This is the pressure-seal/backflow
control function relevant to a stage cyclone return leg; the cyclone body itself
performs gas-solid separation, while the dense solids column in the dipleg
provides the hydraulic seal.

The modeled industrial bed is reported at 331 kPa gauge pressure, a superficial
gas flow of 310,000 Nm3/h, and an initial inventory of 181 tonnes of hematite.
These values describe the FINEX case examined in the paper and are not HyREX
design values.

## Scope boundary

This source directly supports the industrial FINEX-lineage cyclone/dipleg
principle and the distinction between particle separation and gas sealing. It
does not disclose the as-built configuration, pressure profile, seal-leg
geometry, valve logic, or control philosophy selected for the POSCO HyREX
demonstration plant. The HyREX diagram should therefore show the functional
boundary without asserting proprietary dimensions or equipment details.
