# Sitaraman et al. 2026 — reducing species at the HPSR ore interface

- Title: Elucidating key reducing species beyond ions in hydrogen plasma smelting reduction of iron ore
- Journal: Chemical Engineering Science, volume 335, article 124377
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ces.2026.124377
- Publisher page: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0009250926010924

- Collected: 2026-07-28
- Peer review: NLR classifies the record as a peer-reviewed journal article.

## Experimental and modeling scope

- The study used a bench transferred-arc reactor with a 7 kW DC supply,
  100–200 A arc current, 95% Ar–5% H2 gas, and DRI-grade hematite as the
  anodic ore surface.
- A three-dimensional LTE thermal-plasma model was validated against measured
  arc voltage and optical-emission measurements of electron density and plasma
  temperature.
- The validated bulk-arc solution was coupled to a near-wall non-equilibrium
  model covering the micrometre-scale anode sheath and thermal boundary layer.

## Main mechanistic result

- A positive anode-fall region produced electric fields pointing away from the
  ore surface, strongly suppressing fluxes of positive hydrogen-containing
  ions to the anodic ore.
- Non-equilibrium electron-impact processes instead produced abundant neutral
  hydrogen radicals and vibrationally excited H2 near the ore surface.
- At the ore surface, modeled H-radical concentrations were one to two orders
  of magnitude above the other dominant ionic species and rose with current in
  the same direction as the experimentally observed reduction rate.
- The authors therefore conclude that, for this forward-polarity transferred
  arc configuration, neutral non-equilibrium hydrogen species dominate the
  interfacial reduction pathway rather than positive ions.

## Interpretation boundary

- This result corrects an ion-centred mechanistic assumption for the tested
  polarity, gas mixture, current range and anodic ore configuration. It is not a
  universal ranking for every plasma polarity, reactor geometry or gas recipe.
- The study provides mechanistic and diagnostic evidence, not pilot-scale
  throughput, energy consumption, refractory life, electrode consumption,
  availability or product-quality proof.
- The authors identify further validation needs, including controlled
  non-thermal experiments, molecular-scale surface-consumption models and a
  particle-based treatment of the very thin sheath.
