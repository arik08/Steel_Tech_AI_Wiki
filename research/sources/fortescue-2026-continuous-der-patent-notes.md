## Disclosed architecture

- The application describes a continuous electrochemical flow reactor with cathodic and anodic compartments separated by an ion-conducting separator.
- Iron-ore particles with P80 greater than 20 µm are suspended in alkaline catholyte and circulated through the cathodic compartment.
- A potential greater than 1.4 V is applied; the disclosure intentionally permits concurrent hydrogen evolution to limit iron plating on the cathode and keep iron-bearing product as separable solid particles.
- Spent catholyte is sent to a product-separation step; depleted catholyte is replenished with ore and recirculated.
- Disclosed separation options include magnetic, filtration, centrifugal and cyclonic separation.
- The system may use one cell or a multi-cell stack, including header tanks and pumped/gravity circulation.
- Example embodiments describe alkaline concentrations of 20–80 wt%, especially 30–60 wt% NaOH/KOH, and temperatures about 80–130°C.

## Scale-up signal

- The patent maps a plausible continuous solids-circulation and product-recovery architecture beyond a batch cathode-stripping cell.
- It explicitly addresses coarse-enough ore particles, electrolyte recirculation and stacked cells, all relevant to scale-up sensing.

## Interpretation boundary

- Patent disclosure and claims are not evidence that Fortescue built, commissioned or continuously operated the described Stage 2 plant.
- The pending application does not establish TRL 6, product quality, long-duration separator life or commercial energy efficiency.
- Numeric ranges are disclosed embodiments/claims, not representative operating setpoints or achieved commercial performance.
