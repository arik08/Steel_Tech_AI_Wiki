# Prevention of sticking during fluidized-bed reduction of fine iron ore

Guo and co-authors review why iron-ore fines agglomerate and eventually
defluidize during reduction. The most accepted mechanisms involve iron
whiskers and highly active newly formed metallic iron bonding at particle
contacts. Sticking normally appears after metallic iron begins to form and at
an ore- and condition-specific metallization range.

Sticking is influenced by temperature, gas composition and velocity, pressure,
ore grade, particle-size distribution, particle shape, gangue, particle
surface morphology, and reduction history. Smaller particles have more
contact area and lower momentum. Higher-grade low-gangue ore can be more prone
to sticking because gangue separates newly formed iron surfaces.

Lower temperature suppresses sticking but slows reaction and reduces reactor
and gas utilization. Higher gas velocity increases particle momentum and
reduces contact time, but also lowers single-pass gas utilization and raises
circulation energy. Industrial fluidized-bed reduction commonly operates
under pressure to recover some reaction rate.

Mitigation candidates include inert oxide coatings, ore blending, controlled
particle-size distribution or microgranulation, staged low-temperature
pre-reduction, gas-composition control, circulating or conical fluidized beds,
mechanical or acoustic agitation, and control of particle microstructure.
Each measure changes other variables such as gangue load, gas use, power, or
downstream melting burden; no single laboratory recipe applies to all ores.
